vigil is an open-source network sensor for your phone. It shows every connection and DNS lookup each app makes, names the destination, checks it against threat intelligence and flags suspicious behaviour, for security-minded users, researchers and blue teams.

<b>How it works</b>

vigil uses Android's VPN interface as a local loop: a Rust engine inside the app inspects each flow and relays it over ordinary sockets. Nothing is tunnelled to a remote server. It needs <b>no root</b>, installs <b>no certificates</b> and <b>never decrypts TLS or QUIC</b>. Destination names come from DNS answers, the TLS ClientHello (SNI), QUIC Initial packets and plain-HTTP Host headers.

<b>Features</b>

<ul>
<li>Per-app attribution of every connection and DNS lookup, including background system components</li>
<li>Destination names without decryption, JA4 fingerprints for TLS and QUIC clients, network (ASN) labels</li>
<li>Threat intelligence: built-in feeds (HaGeZi TIF, abuse.ch, Phishing Army, Spamhaus DROP, tracker and telemetry lists), custom URLs including authenticated MISP exports, TAXII 2.1 and JA4 lists</li>
<li>Spyware and stalkerware: indicator packs (MVT, Amnesty International, Echap) block known spyware servers; an offline health check compares installed apps and history with them</li>
<li>Tracker labels: the company behind each destination (AdGuard companiesdb) and the tracker companies each app contacts</li>
<li>Blocking: DNS sinkholing (including CNAME-cloaked trackers), connections refused by IP, SNI or HTTP Host, custom allow/deny rules</li>
<li>Per-app firewall: block an app always, on Wi-Fi, on mobile data, in the background or with the screen off, and allow or block a domain for one app only</li>
<li>Behavioural detection: beaconing, hard-coded DNS servers, encrypted DNS (DoH/DoT/DoQ), unusual background uploads, optional new-destination and new-network alerts</li>
<li>Encrypted DNS (DoT or DoH), and chaining through your WireGuard server or a SOCKS5 proxy such as Orbot</li>
<li>Optional packet capture in memory, exported as PCAPng for Wireshark or streamed live over PCAP-over-IP</li>
<li>Optional SIEM export: ECS-shaped JSON over syslog (UDP, TCP or TLS with optional mutual TLS), HTTP NDJSON, Splunk HEC or Elasticsearch bulk</li>
<li>Always-on VPN support and a Quick Settings tile</li>
</ul>

<b>Privacy: what leaves the device</b>

History stays in the app's private storage, excluded from backups, and is deleted after 7 days by default. Only the method and Host of plain-HTTP requests are recorded; never paths, headers or bodies. Packet capture is off by default and keeps packets in memory only.

vigil itself contacts only:
<ul>
<li>the publishers of the feeds and data sources you leave on, with plain HTTPS downloads: threat feeds daily (four on by default: HaGeZi TIF and three abuse.ch lists), spyware packs (GitHub), the IP-to-ASN table (iptoasn.com, weekly) and tracker labels (GitHub, weekly). Publishers see your IP address, nothing about your traffic. Each can be turned off.</li>
<li>the SIEM collector you configure, only if you turn on export.</li>
</ul>
There are no analytics, no ads and no accounts.

<b>Limitations</b>

<ul>
<li>Requires Android 10 or later.</li>
<li>Android allows one VPN at a time, so vigil cannot run alongside another VPN app (it can route its traffic through a WireGuard server or a SOCKS5 proxy instead).</li>
<li>With Private DNS in strict mode, DNS lookups are encrypted before vigil sees them; names then come from TLS/QUIC/HTTP only.</li>
<li>Encrypted Client Hello hides the real destination name; vigil sees only the provider's public name.</li>
<li>ICMP (ping) is not relayed.</li>
</ul>

<b>Permissions</b>

VPN service (to inspect traffic locally), internet, notifications, a foreground service, visibility of installed apps (to name the app behind each connection, and for the spyware health check) and optional usage access (to tell foreground from background traffic).
